# The Reservoir of Resilience: Engineering the Common Pool

*A dispatch from the Threshold-Based Flow Funding (TBFF) experiment.*

We’ve made a fundamental shift in the plumbing of our mycelial network. We are moving from a peer-to-peer web of pipes to a **Common Reservoir**. 

In this model, the **Common Pool** acts as the system's "heart," mediating the pulse of nutrients across the 6-node grove.

## The Shared Tank: From P2P to Hub-and-Spoke

While the **Anderson Mechanism** remains the brain, the Pool is now the stomach. When an individual node reaches its **Saturation Point**, its excess nutrients don't just flow to a specific neighbor—they flow into the **Common Pool**.

### The Seniority Waterfall (Mediated)

This centralization isn't about control; it's about **Resilience**. By routing all nutrients through a central clearinghouse, we can mathematically enforce our most important "Law of the Grove":

**The Pool prioritizes the survival of the many over the growth of the few.**

No node can draw nutrients for growth until the Pool has verified that every single node in the grove has hit its **Sustainability Floor**. The Pool acts as an automated social safety net, capturing the "shadow" of the tallest trees and translocating it to the thirsty saplings.

## The Collective Reservoir

The **"Mycelial Pulse"** is now a collective heartbeat. The Pool creates a buffer against the stochastic shocks of the external economy, ensuring that even in a drought, the grove's metabolic rate remains stable.

The nutrients are pooling. The pulse is steady. The grove is resilient.
