## Summary (from author's Substack announcement)

This paper introduces the **Commitment Pooling Protocol (CPP)** — a formal framework proposing that many diverse institutions share a common operational grammar underneath:

- Savings groups, grain banks, rotating labor groups, insurance pools, clearing houses, deposit systems, and mutual-credit networks all ask variants of four questions:
  1. What commitments are allowed in?
  2. How are those commitments valued?
  3. What limits protect the pool and its members?
  4. How can commitments be exchanged, settled, repaired, or routed?

### Key Concepts

- **Commitment Pooling**: A grammar for linking institutions by helping them recognize, value, limit, exchange, and repair commitments across different contexts
- **Route Graphs**: Pathways enabling different kinds of institutions (mutual-aid groups, food networks, savings circles, voucher systems, labor-sharing groups) to become interoperable parts of a wider ecology of commitments
- **Bounded Framework**: The paper marks explicit limits — some institutions cannot be represented if they depend on open-ended legal discretion, political authority, or social judgment that cannot be written into explicit rules

### Relevance to TBFF

Both CPP and TBFF deal with:

- **Flow-based resource allocation** across networks
- **Threshold/limit mechanisms** that protect participants
- **Recursive redistribution** according to participant preferences
- **Sustainability of contributors** in decentralized systems

### Examples Covered in Paper

- Deposits, secured lending, clearing
- Rotating savings (ROSCAs), rotating labor
- Grain banks, reserve-support repair
